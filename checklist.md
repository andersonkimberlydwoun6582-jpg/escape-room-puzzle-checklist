# Escape room puzzle checklist

## Core loop

- The player understands the main objective within the first screen.
- The first interaction teaches the basic rule without a long tutorial.
- Each puzzle changes the player’s understanding of the room, route, or lock.
- The player receives feedback after every meaningful attempt.
- The win condition is visible or strongly implied.

## Clue design

- Every code, key, switch, or path has at least one discoverable clue.
- Clues are placed near the action they explain or are repeated in a memorable way.
- Red herrings are optional and never block the main path.
- Visual clues use contrast, position, sound, or repeated symbols.
- Text clues are short enough to remember while playing.

## Difficulty pacing

- The first puzzle is forgiving.
- Later puzzles combine ideas already introduced.
- The final challenge feels like a synthesis rather than a random trick.
- If the game is short, avoid multi-step inventory chains that require backtracking through every screen.

## Fail states

- Failed attempts explain what did not work.
- The player can retry without restarting the whole game.
- Timers, guards, and hazards add tension but do not hide essential information.
- Dead ends are brief and readable.

## Walkthrough support

- A walkthrough should list the objective, controls, puzzle order, and optional hints.
- Hint sections should reveal information gradually.
- Code-status notes should say whether codes are current, expired, unknown, or not used.
